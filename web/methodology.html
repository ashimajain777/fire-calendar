How large is the conversion error?

Each conversion was tested by leaving one year out, fitting the ratios on the other years, and predicting the year left out. The error is the median absolute percentage difference between predicted and observed counts. Band coverage is the share of observed values that fall inside the band; a 10th–90th percentile band should cover about 80 %.

Which version of the ratios is used

Two versions were fitted: one ratio per category and month for all of India ("no region"), and one per 5° box, category and month ("region"). The table compares them on whole-India monthly totals.

Fire types and the static-source mask

Every hotspot is labelled, never deleted. The label comes from the FIRMS type field, the static-source mask, and the land cover at the hotspot's location. Only the four landscape categories are shown by default.

Static-source mask

The mask marks 0.01° cells (about 1.1 km) that hold a fixed heat source such as a gas flare, a steel plant or a brick kiln. It is built from the last 5 complete years of data. A cell joins the mask if either rule holds:

  • FIRMS itself tagged at least 3 detections there as a static land source or as a volcano.
  • Fire was detected there on at least 30 different days in at least 3 of the 5 years, spread over at least 6 calendar months in one year. The month spread keeps crop-residue cells, which burn on many days in two short seasons, out of the mask.

Data gaps

Data sources and versions

Hotspots are NASA FIRMS active fire detections: MODIS Collection 6.1 (Terra and Aqua, 1 km) and VIIRS 375 m (S-NPP and NOAA-20). Low-confidence detections are kept in the files but are not counted.

Known limitations

  • India only.
  • The static-source mask is built from the last 5 years and applied to all years. A kiln or plant built recently can label fires from years before it existed as "industrial".
  • Land cover is a single year (the product is named under data sources above). Land that changed use over the record is labelled by what it was in that year.
  • Ground-reported fires are not covered. India has no open nationwide dataset of them, so only satellite detections are counted.
  • Areas are built from a 0.1° grid (cells of about 11 km).
  • Season start and end dates are accurate to about ±10 days.
  • The Mumbai High offshore flares are not in the FIRMS India country file, so they are absent here.
